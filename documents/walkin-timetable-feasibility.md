## 1. Verdict (one page)

**Yes — there is a real, recurring walk-in market in these towns.** Enough events exist to power a **daily/weekly timetable**, especially if we accept **all experience bands** (fresher + experienced) and **voice + non-voice**.

What changes the product from “another social poster”:

| Commodity poster | Walk-in timetable (us) |
|---|---|
| “Company X is hiring” | “**Show up here on this date at this time**” |
| Scraped job card / apply link that may lie | Date + venue + eligibility + **source URL you can open** |
| Flood of stale openings | Future-date filter; past dates drop off the board |

**Volume is not “thousands of unique drives tomorrow.”** It is **dozens of distinct events per week across the four cities**, plus **rolling multi-day windows** (same venue open for days/weeks). That is enough for a timetable product; it is **not** enough if we pretend every LinkedIn “walkin” job card is a unique tomorrow-event (many are expired, duplicated, or skill tags without a date).

## 4. What the data says about “timetable” vs “job post hunter”

### 4.1 The content is already timetable-shaped

Almost every good walk-in post already carries:

- Date (or date range)
- Time window
- Venue / map
- Experience band
- Documents to carry
- Sometimes registration / Google Form

We do **not** need to invent the format. We need to **collect, normalize, de-dupe, drop past dates, and publish a calendar**.

### 4.2 Noise we must kill (or the timetable lies)

| Noise | Example from this sweep | Timetable rule |
|---|---|---|
| Expired still indexed | Infosys BPM “31st July” still on Bengaluru walkin SERP | Drop if event end &lt; today |
| Duplicate reposts | Same HCL Chennai voice drive ×10 cards | Dedupe on company+venue+date |
| Experienced-only tagged as fresher bait | Quest Global “2026 batch not eligible” | Label experience honestly |
| Voice flood | HCL / Conduent / Omega voice megas | Keep on full timetable; filter later for non-voice niche |
| Aggregator rewrite risk | foundthejob / telugucareershub copy LinkedIn | Prefer primary LinkedIn / company post; aggregator = secondary |
| Kochi thin LinkedIn Jobs | Only 8 cards | Must include posts + placement digests (Manimaran-style) |

### 4.3 Source ranking for MVP (still no code)

1. **LinkedIn Posts** (`#WalkInDrive`, city + “walk in”, recruiter shares) — best date/venue prose  
2. **LinkedIn Jobs** title keyword `walkin` / `walk-in` — good for Infosys BPM / HCL / TCS dated titles  
3. **Weekly placement digests** (e.g. Manimaran V post covering 17–23 Aug) — high-density multi-city  
4. **Aggregators** (Freshershunt, foundthejob, Telugu Careers Hub) — discovery, then verify against primary  
5. **Later:** company career pages / Naukri walk-in tabs / Instagram (out of MVP)

### 10.1 Reading the pattern (2 data points, directional not final)

1. **Shorter runtime wins.** CIEL's 8-sec cut held viewers to ~50% completion and a near-baseline skip rate; Cognizant's 13-sec cut lost most viewers by the 4-sec mark (~31% completion). → **Keep future walk-in reels at 8–10 sec, front-load company/city/date in the first 2–3 seconds** since most viewers never reach the back half.
2. **Distribution is not the problem.** 81.7% non-follower views and ~78% of traffic from Reels tab + Explore means Instagram's algorithm is already willing to push "walk-in alert" content to strangers. The bottleneck is **turning a view into a comment**, not reach.
3. **Shares (4) + saves (3) with zero comments is a real signal, just the wrong shape for the goal.** People found this useful enough to privately forward or bookmark — that *is* product-market signal — but it doesn't count toward the public 100-comment bar and gives us zero visible social proof for new visitors.
4. **Zero comments on both reels = the CTA is too weak or absent.** Need a one-word, zero-effort comment bait tied to the walk-in itself, e.g. *"Comment 'DONE' if you're walking in tomorrow"* or *"Comment your city 📍 for tomorrow's list"* — low friction, and doubles as a lead-capture signal for whichever city to prioritize next.
